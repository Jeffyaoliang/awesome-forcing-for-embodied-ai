## Research contribution

The central intervention is independently sampling diffusion noise levels for each position in a causal sequence. This allows a flexible temporal prediction horizon and combines autoregressive conditioning with diffusion guidance. The paper additionally derives a variational lower-bound interpretation.

**Evidence:** [Sections 3.1–3.2, Algorithms 1–2](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01392v4), [Introduction](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01392v4).

## Supported strengths

1. The method is specified algorithmically with per-token random noise levels in Algorithm 1 and a configurable sampling matrix in Algorithm 2 (Section 3.2).
2. The experiments span video, planning, robot imitation/visuomotor control and forecasting, supporting a general sequence-modeling rather than robot-only interpretation (Section 4).
3. The paper explicitly discusses its theoretical objective and subsequence ELBO (Appendix A).

## Supported limitations / questions

1. **Scaling is untested in the reported original work.** The authors explicitly state the causal implementation is RNN-based and do not investigate internet-scale training, while suggesting transformer scaling as future work (Section 5, *Limitations*). This is a documented limitation, **not** proof the method cannot scale.
2. **Separate mechanism from compute.** Per-token schedules, different guidance schemes, and architecture choices are coupled across tasks. A future benchmark should control backbone, planning budget and conditioning before attributing gains solely to the per-token training schedule. This is a proposed *review question*, not a verified methodological flaw.
3. **Robot transfer should not be inferred from video fidelity alone.** The paper does report robot experiments in Section 4.4, but the general long-video results and downstream action effectiveness should remain distinct metrics.
