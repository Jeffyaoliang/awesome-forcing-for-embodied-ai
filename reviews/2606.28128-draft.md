## Research contribution

A physics-focused fine-tuning method for robotic video-world simulation. It supervises model features with pixel-trajectory alignment and semantic inter-region relation alignment, focusing loss on physically informative areas.

**Evidence:** [Abstract and method](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.28128v1), Section 4.4 and Tables 3–6.

## Supported strengths

1. The paper isolates two physics losses: in Table 4 (Wan2.2-TI2V-5B), fine-tuning baseline R-Bench average is 44.8; pixel-only 47.2; semantic-only 46.2; combined 47.5. The Wan2.2-I2V-A14B comparison is 57.9 vs 62.0 (Table 4).
2. Physics-region focus has a separate ablation: baseline 44.8, without region focus 46.0, with region focus 47.5 (Table 5).
3. WorldArena closed-loop planning with the listed base model reports 16% versus 24% after PhysisForcing (Table 3). This is an author-reported gain of **8 percentage points**, not independent replication.

## Limitations / review questions

1. **Backbone ceiling is acknowledged by the authors.** Appendix F states that Wan/Cosmos backbone limitations in world knowledge and long-horizon reasoning constrain what fine-tuning can fix.
2. **Robustness of closed-loop gain needs uncertainty reporting.** Table 3 gives success rates for two tasks, but the presented table alone does not establish seed variability or statistical confidence. Check the experimental protocol and trial count before judging significance. This is a *verification question*, not a claim that the paper omitted all such information.
3. **Physical metrics are not task success.** R-Bench changes should remain distinct from WorldArena and downstream policy outcomes; the latter need their own protocol and baseline controls.
